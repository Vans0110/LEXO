# Chapter 10: Numbers & Money

## Цель главы

Использовать числа, цены и количество, оплачивать покупки и вести простой
бюджет.

## Ядро

- Слова: money, cash, card, coin, note, wallet, price, cost, pay, spend, save,
  cheap, expensive, change, receipt, total.
- Данные: числа до 1000, суммы, скидки, простые количества.
- Грамматика: `how much`, `how many`, исчисляемые количества.

## Book 1: The Lost Wallet

Target: wallet, cash, card, lose, find, owner, inside, return.
Recycle: market, price, photo, number.

Chris goes to a market with fifty dollars. He buys apples for four dollars, bread for three, and vegetables for eight.

Then he wants coffee. When he reaches into his pocket, his wallet is gone.

Chris walks back past every market table. He checks near the fruit, under a bench, and beside the bread shop.

An older woman holds up a brown wallet.

"Is this yours?"

Chris describes it. "There are thirty-five dollars in cash, two notes, three coins, one bank card, and a family photo inside."

The woman opens it. The amount, card, and photo are correct.

"I found it near the tomato table," she says. "I wanted to return it to its owner."

Chris thanks her and offers to buy her coffee. Two coffees cost six dollars.

After paying, Chris puts the wallet in a zipped pocket because he does not want to lose it again. He came to the market to spend money, but the most valuable thing he found was an honest person.

## Book 2: The Phone Fund

Target: save, spend, goal, each, week, amount, enough.
Recycle: money, cost, work, number.

Lina's goal is a phone that costs two hundred and forty dollars. She has one hundred and twenty dollars.

She makes a simple savings plan. Each week, she saves fifteen dollars in a blue box. She writes the amount on a chart.

Week one: one hundred and thirty-five dollars. Week two: one hundred and fifty dollars. Week three: one hundred and sixty-five dollars.

Sometimes Lina wants to spend the money on coffee or clothes. She asks herself, "Do I need this today?"

After eight weeks, the box has two hundred and forty dollars. At the shop, the phone is now on sale for two hundred and twenty, and Lina pays by card.

Lina buys it and still has twenty dollars.

She does not spend the extra money. She starts a new line on the chart: `Bicycle fund — twenty dollars`.

Saving is slow, but Lina likes watching a small amount become enough. The chart helps her see every step.

## Book 3: Three Prices

Target: compare, cheaper, cheapest, more, less, quality, choice.
Recycle: price, cost, buy, shop.

Owen's goal is a desk lamp. The shop has a dozen lamps, and he compares three of them.

The first costs eighteen dollars. It is small and uses a lot of electricity.

The second costs twenty-five dollars. It is brighter and uses less electricity.

The third costs forty dollars. It is the largest and most expensive, and it has three light levels.

Owen has thirty dollars. The first lamp is the cheapest, but the second has better quality and is still inside his budget.

He buys the second lamp and pays with a fifty-dollar note. His change is twenty-five dollars.

At home, the lamp fits beside his computer. It gives enough light for reading and uses less power than the cheap lamp.

The lowest price was not the best choice. Owen spent seven dollars more, but he bought the lamp he really needed.

## Book 4: Market Day

Target: total, kilo, half, dozen, receipt, count, weigh.
Recycle: fruit, vegetables, price, cash.

Rosa has a twenty-dollar budget for the market. She needs one kilo of potatoes, half a kilo of tomatoes, six eggs, and twelve apples.

The seller weighs the potatoes and tomatoes. They cost five dollars together. A dozen apples costs six dollars. Six eggs cost three dollars.

"What is the total?" Rosa asks.

"Fourteen dollars."

Rosa compares the prices, counts her cash and coins, and pays. The seller gives her six dollars in change and a receipt.

At another table, strawberries cost seven dollars. They are not cheap and are too expensive for Rosa because she has only six dollars left.

She buys a smaller box for four dollars and keeps two dollars for the bus.

At home, Rosa checks the receipt and her shopping list. She has every needed item, one extra box of fruit, and exactly enough money for the journey home.

## Book 5: The Cafe Bill

Target: bill, share, equal, tip, total, mistake, check.
Recycle: order, menu, dollar, pay.

Four friends eat at a cafe. Their food costs:

Maya: eight dollars. Jon: ten dollars. Sara: nine dollars. Lee: eleven dollars.

The bill says forty-three dollars. The friends add their prices.

"Eight plus ten plus nine plus eleven is thirty-eight," Sara says.

They check the bill. It includes a five-dollar cake, but nobody ordered cake. The waiter removes the mistake.

The friends add a four-dollar tip. Half the group checks the new total: forty-two dollars.

"Can we share it equally?" Jon asks.

Forty-two cannot make four equal shares in whole dollars. Each person pays ten dollars, and Maya and Lee each add one more dollar.

Lee takes a wallet from his bag, and the waiter gives them a correct receipt.

Before they leave, Sara checks the numbers one last time. A simple addition saved them five dollars and found a mistake.
