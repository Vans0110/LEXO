## Book 1: The Summer We Moved

Target: move house, pack, unpack, neighbourhood, settle in, memory, rent, removal van.
Recycle: home, furniture, neighbour, directions, weather.

When I was fourteen, my family had to move house because my mother found a new job. We rented a small house in another town, two hours from our old home.

For a week, we packed everything after dinner. Mum wrote a number on every box and made a list:

`one — kitchen` `two — books` `three — bathroom` `four — first night`

The fourth box had towels, cups, phone chargers, and clean clothes. It was the most important box, but nobody saw where it went.

On moving day, rain was falling while the removal van was crossing the town. When we arrived, the new neighbourhood looked grey and unfamiliar. Our house was between a bakery and a family with two young children.

We began to unpack, but Box four was missing. My father searched the van. Mum checked every room. I was looking under a table when our new neighbour knocked on the door.

"Is this yours?" she asked. The box was in her hall. The workers had carried it to the wrong house because both buildings had blue doors.

We laughed, but we were too tired to unpack everything. We made one simple decision: first, we would finish the living room. We put the sofa, lamp, and kettle there, made tea, and ate bread from the bakery next door.

That evening, the children from the other house showed me the park and the bus stop. The neighbourhood did not feel friendly in the rain, but the people were.

It took several weeks to settle in, and the move changed our daily life. For a long time, my strongest memory was not the journey or the missing box. It was our first evening, when four people sat among fifty closed boxes and decided that one comfortable room was enough for one night.

## Book 2: A Photograph from School

Target: recognise, classmate, uniform, event, memory, remember, school fair, embarrassed.
Recycle: appearance, clothes, teacher, date, feelings.

Last winter, Nina was helping her parents clean a cupboard when she found an old school photograph. About thirty children were standing in front of a table with cakes and handmade toys.

The date on the back was May eighteenth, twenty fourteen. Under it, somebody had written: `Spring School Fair — Class four B`.

Nina recognised her teacher at once. Mrs Bell was wearing the same red glasses that she used to wear in class. Nina also recognised several classmates, although they looked much younger.

"Where are you?" her mother asked.

Nina studied the photograph. One girl had Nina's dark hair, but she was wearing a green uniform. Nina remembered that her school uniform was blue.

"Maybe I was absent," she said.

Then she noticed a short boy behind the cake table. His face was covered with white cream, and everyone around him was laughing.

"That boy looks like your brother," Mum said.

"He was only six. He wasn't in my class."

Nina took the photograph to Mrs Bell, who still lived near the school. The old teacher looked at it and smiled.

"The child with the cream is not a boy," she said. "That is you."

During the fair, Nina had been carrying a large cake when a football hit the table. She tried to save the cake, but it landed on her face. Her long hair was under a cap, and the green clothes belonged to the school play.

At first, Nina felt embarrassed. Then she remembered what had happened, and the whole memory returned: the cold cream, her classmates' surprised faces, and the prize they won for the best cake.

Mrs Bell found the event notice in an old folder. It included one final line: `Thank you to Nina Reed for saving half the cake.`

Nina put the notice and photograph together. She still did not recognise the child immediately, but now she remembered exactly how that child felt.

## Book 3: My First Real Job

Target: apply for, interview, experience, responsibility, customer, mistake, improve, application form, confident.
Recycle: schedule, shift, manager, email, money.

At nineteen, Omar wanted to earn his own money during the summer. He saw an online notice from a busy cafe:

`Weekend assistant wanted. No experience needed. Morning and afternoon shifts.`

Omar saw the notice as an opportunity and decided to apply for the job. He completed the application form, but one question worried him: `Why should we choose you?`

He wrote, `I learn quickly, and I take responsibility for my mistakes.`

Two days later, the manager invited him to an interview. While Omar was waiting outside the office, two experienced workers were carrying six plates at once. He suddenly felt less confident.

The manager did not ask him to carry plates. She asked about his schedule, school projects, and experience with customers. Omar explained that he had helped at his uncle's market table.

"Can you start on Saturday?" she asked.

His first shift began at eight. A worker showed him how to write orders and use the payment screen. At ten, the cafe became crowded.

One customer ordered tea without milk and a cheese sandwich. Omar wrote the order correctly, but he carried it to the wrong table. Another customer had already taken one bite from the sandwich when Omar noticed his mistake.

He wanted to hide in the kitchen. Instead, he told the manager at once. She apologised to both customers, replaced the food, and then asked Omar what had happened.

"I checked the order, but I did not check the table number," he said.

For the rest of the shift, Omar repeated each table number before leaving the kitchen. The result was clear: his work became slower for a while, but it also became more accurate.

At the end of the day, the manager sent him the next week's schedule.

"Was the mistake terrible?" Omar asked.

"No," she said. "A repeated mistake is a problem. A corrected mistake is experience."

Omar left tired and carrying less confidence than he had wanted. But on his next shift, he knew exactly what he needed to improve.

## Book 4: The Day the Lights Went Out

Target: power cut, electricity, torch, candle, suddenly, check, return, fuse box, corridor.
Recycle: apartment, phone, neighbour, safe, battery.

Maya was cooking soup on an electric cooker when the kitchen light suddenly went out. The cooker stopped, the fridge became quiet, and music disappeared from the living room.

At first, she thought a lamp had broken. Then she looked through the window. The buildings across the street were dark too.

"It is a power cut," she said.

Her phone battery was at twelve percent. Maya used its light to find a torch in a drawer. Before leaving the apartment, she checked that the cooker was off. She did not touch the fuse box because the whole building had lost electricity.

While this was happening, she heard her older neighbour, Mr Lee, calling from the corridor. His torch had no batteries, and he could not see the steps clearly.

Maya helped him return to his apartment. They found two candles, but she put them on a metal plate away from the curtains. Then her phone received a message from the building manager:

`Power cut in three streets. Workers are checking the problem. Electricity should return before nine o'clock. Please do not use the lift.`

It was only seven thirty, so Maya and Mr Lee sat by the window. Her soup was half-cooked, and his dinner was still in the freezer.

"I have bread, cheese, and tomatoes," he said.

"I have fruit and cold chicken."

They made a simple dinner together. Other neighbours were talking in the corridor, and someone was playing a guitar downstairs. Without televisions or computers, the building sounded unusually alive.

At eight forty-five, the lights returned. The fridge started again, and the lift made a soft sound.

Maya went home and finished her soup. Before bed, she charged her phone and put fresh batteries beside the torch.

The power cut lasted only seventy-five minutes. It stopped her dinner, but it also introduced her to a neighbour she had passed silently for two years.

## Book 5: Three Small Turning Points

Target: turning point, decision, opportunity, choose, regret, change, ordinary, volunteer.
Recycle: job, friendship, study, travel, confidence, memory.

For a long time, Daniel thought a turning point had to be a dramatic event. He imagined a new job in another country or one decision that changed everything in a day.

Then he looked at three dates in his old calendar.

The first date was March sixth. Daniel was walking home from work when he saw a notice for an evening photography course. He almost passed it because he was tired. The course cost more than he wanted to spend, but he chose to enter the building and ask one question.

The teacher showed him the classroom and offered a free first lesson. Daniel joined the course. He never became a professional photographer, but he met Maya there. Their friendship began while they were taking pictures of the same rainy street.

The second date was June twenty-first. Maya invited him to volunteer at a community festival. Daniel planned to refuse because he did not know anyone there. While he was writing his reply, he remembered the photography lesson and changed `No` to `Yes`.

At the festival, a food seller needed help with photographs for a website. Daniel took the pictures. A week later, the seller offered him a small paid job. It was not much money, but it was his first opportunity to use his new skill outside the classroom.

The third date was October second. Daniel received an email about a weekend trip for young photographers. He wanted to go, but he was afraid of travelling with strangers. This time, he answered before he could think of ten reasons to stay home.

During the trip, Daniel was taking a photograph near a lake when the group leader asked to use it in an exhibition. Daniel felt proud, but he also remembered the notice on March sixth.

He did not regret any of the three decisions. None looked important at first. One was a question, one was a short message, and one was an email reply.

Each experience began with an ordinary choice. Daniel compared the result of each choice and wrote beside the three dates:

`Small choices can open large doors.`

His life did not change in one moment. It changed because three ordinary moments gave him an opportunity, and three times he chose to step closer.
