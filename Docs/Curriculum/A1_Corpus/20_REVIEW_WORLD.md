## Book 1: The New Neighbour's Saturday

Target: review only.

Nora's new neighbour, Alex, moves into Apartment seven on Saturday morning. He is twenty-two and comes from Greece.

Nora helps him carry two boxes upstairs. One has plates, cups, a kettle, a knife, and a pan for the cooker. The other has books, clothes, and a lamp. Nora puts the clothes in the wardrobe and leaves a clean towel in the bathroom.

A third box moves.

"That is Olive," Alex says. Inside is a calm rabbit with soft brown fur.

They put Olive's cage near the window. Alex adds fresh hay and fills her food bowl. He explains how to feed and care for her.

At noon, Nora and Alex go to the market. They buy bread, pasta, vegetables, chicken, fruit, tomato sauce, and juice. Alex checks every price and keeps the receipt. Heavy rain begins, so they take Bus twelve home.

In the evening, Alex cooks dinner and sends Nora a message: `Pasta at seven?`

They eat at the new table and talk about family, work, and hobbies. Olive sits beside them and eats a piece of carrot.

"Your rabbit already looks at home," Nora says.

Alex looks around the apartment. The boxes are open, the lamp is on, and a friend is sitting across the table.

"I do too," he says.

## Book 2: The Longest Morning

Target: review only.

Mia gets up at seven. Her alarm should have rung at six thirty. It is a weekday, and she has a job interview at nine.

She takes a shower and chooses a cotton shirt, black trousers, a blue jacket, and a pair of leather shoes. There is no milk, so she eats a banana and takes water.

Bus six is late. Mia takes Metro Line two instead. Her phone battery is almost empty.

She arrives at the office at eight fifty-five. The manager asks her to complete a short computer exam. A guard shows her the meeting room and gives her a folder and a pen. Mia opens the email, downloads the attachment, and connects her headphones. Then the laptop goes offline.

"Do not worry," the manager says. He brings a charger and checks the internet connection.

When the screen comes back, Mia clicks the file and finishes the exam. She uses the keyboard to write about a university project and draws a chart with a ruler. The manager asks about her lessons and work experience.

At ten, the interview ends. Her phone still has one percent power. A message arrives before the screen goes dark:

`Thank you, Mia. We would like you to start on Monday.`

Mia reads it twice. The long morning has a short answer: yes.

## Book 3: A Weekend at the Lake

Target: review only.

Four friends plan a weekend at a lake. They check the weather, book rooms, and buy train tickets.

Ben packs sandwiches, a dozen biscuits, and juice. Sara brings a first-aid box. Leo brings a map, and Maya brings games.

On Saturday, the sky is clear, but dark clouds, wind, and dry autumn leaves soon cover the path. It is a cold season, and the temperature falls quickly. Near a bridge, a friendly dog runs toward them. Its lead is broken, and there is a phone number on its collar.

Maya calls the owner. While they wait, the dog suddenly runs after a wild bird. Ben follows it, falls on the wet road, and hurts his arm and one finger. His ankle also hurts. He has some pain and a headache, but he is not dizzy.

Sara cleans his hand, puts a bandage on his finger, and tells him to rest.

The dog's owner arrives with her son. She thanks the friends and gives them local cakes.

That evening, thunder starts a storm. The friends stay in the hotel, drink tea, and share the cakes. Ben's arm feels better.

Outside, rain falls on the lake. In winter, the water may become ice. In spring, it will melt again. Tonight, the friends are simply happy to be warm and safe.

## Book 4: The Holiday Music Night

Target: review only.

The community centre team plans a holiday music night to collect money. Rosa creates an online group and sends an invitation.

Mina accepts at once. She will sing. Omar would like to play the guitar, and Nick offers to decorate the hall. Sara does not reply.

Rosa sends another message, but it has an unknown email attachment. She does not click or forward it. She deletes the message and calls Sara instead.

"I sent the wrong file," Sara says. "I feel upset because my song is not ready."

"Come and practise with us," Rosa says. "You do not have to sing alone."

On Friday, they connect a computer to two speakers. The internet stops, but Omar has the music offline. His guitar is the first instrument. Nick hangs lights, and Mina paints a sign for the gift game.

Sara is nervous before the first song. Her friends stand beside her, and the room becomes quiet. Then she begins.

In their free time before the show, some friends play a sport outside and write the score on the sign. A cinema club prepares a short comedy. At midnight, nobody watches the fireworks. They are still inside, singing the last song together.

Sara laughs after it ends. "Next year, please invite me again."

The final song becomes a new holiday tradition.

## Book 5: One Year Later

Target: review only.

A year after his first class, Daniel returns to Room twelve. His teacher welcomes him.

He remembers his name tag, the wrong room number, and his first conversation with Kate. During the year, he learned to ask directions and explain a problem.

This happened this morning: Daniel found a bird on the ground. It had hurt one wing, and a grey feather lay beside it. The bird tried to escape, but it could not fly.

Daniel called an animal rescue centre. A worker arrived with a box and said the bird was safe. She cared for many animals there: a cat, a dog, a fish, and even an old horse. An active pet dog with a long tail waited beside its owner.

"I hope to work with animals someday," Daniel told her.

Now a new student stands near the classroom door. She looks nervous.

"Hello," Daniel says. "My name is Daniel. Are you in the English class?"

"Yes. I am Sofia."

Daniel shows her Room twelve and helps her find a seat. The teacher gives everyone a new book marked `A two`.

"Is A two difficult?" Sofia asks.

Daniel remembers how strange English once felt. His next goal is to improve his speaking, but he knows that progress is possible.

"It is new," he says. "That is different."
