Three Prices

Owen's goal is a desk lamp. The shop has a dozen lamps, and he compares three of them.

The first costs eighteen dollars. It is small and uses a lot of electricity.

The second costs twenty-five dollars. It is brighter and uses less electricity.

The third costs forty dollars. It is the largest and most expensive, and it has three light levels.

Owen has thirty dollars. The first lamp is the cheapest, but the second has better quality and is still inside his budget.

He buys the second lamp and pays with a fifty-dollar note. His change is twenty-five dollars.

At home, the lamp fits beside his computer. It gives enough light for reading and uses less power than the cheap lamp.

The lowest price was not the best choice. Owen spent seven dollars more, but he bought the lamp he really needed.
