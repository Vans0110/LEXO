# Chapter 16: Holidays & Special Days

## Цель главы

Назвать праздник, дату и традицию, пригласить человека и подготовить событие.

## Ядро

- Слова: holiday, birthday, party, invitation, guest, gift, card, cake,
  candle, decorate, celebrate, invite, accept, refuse, bring.
- Данные: даты, месяцы, время события, количество гостей.
- Грамматика: готовые модели `Would you like to...?`, `Can you come?`

## Book 1: The Birthday Invitation

Target: invitation, invite, guest, party, accept, date, place.
Recycle: birthday, message, time, address.

Lena is having a birthday party on Saturday, September twelfth. She writes a short invitation:

`Please come to my birthday party at four o'clock. eighteen King Street, Apartment three.`

She uses it to invite eight friends. Six accept. One friend must refuse because of work, and another is visiting family.

Each guest plans to bring a small gift or birthday card.

Tom replies, "Can I come at five? My class finishes at four thirty."

"Yes," Lena writes. "We are eating cake at five thirty."

The friend who must work feels disappointed and sad. Lena is sorry and does not ignore the message. "I will include you next time," she writes. Her friend forgives the change.

On Saturday, Lena puts a paper list by the door. Every guest writes a name when they arrive.

She puts one candle on the cake before the guests arrive.

At five, there are only five names. Lena feels nervous and lonely for a moment. Then Tom and two other friends arrive together, and she is surprised, excited, and happy.

"Now there are eight guests," Lena says.

"Nine," Tom answers. He points to Lena. "The birthday person is here too."

The invitation gave everyone the same date, time, and place. The party gives them a reason to be together.

## Book 2: Twelve Candles

Target: candle, light, blow, wish, count, turn, special.
Recycle: cake, number, family, birthday.

Mila's younger brother Ben is twelve today. His grandmother helps the family put twelve candles on his cake.

The holiday present beside the cake is wrapped in blue paper.

Grandma counts them. "One, two, three... eleven. We need one more."

There are no candles left in the box. Mila finds a small candle in a kitchen drawer, but it is blue and the others are white.

"That is fine," Ben says. "Blue is my favourite colour."

It is Ben's turn. Dad lights the candles, everyone sings, and Ben closes his eyes to make a wish.

He blows once. Ten candles go out. He blows again, and the last two go dark.

"What did you wish for?" Mila asks.

"I cannot tell you. Then it may not come true."

Later, Ben gives Mila the blue candle.

"Keep it for your birthday," he says. "A different candle made my cake special."

Grandma calls it a new family tradition.

## Book 3: New Year Next Door

Target: New Year, midnight, decorate, tradition, fireworks, calendar.
Recycle: neighbour, food, music, family.

Nora celebrates New Year with her neighbours for the first time. They decorate the hall with paper stars and lights.

Children decorate cards with pictures of midnight fireworks.

Mrs Park brings rice cakes. Amir brings bread and cheese. Nora makes a salad from her home country.

At eleven thirty, everyone writes one hope for the new year on a card. They do not write their names.

At midnight, fireworks begin outside. The neighbours count the last ten seconds together.

After midnight, Mr Lee reads the hope cards. One says, `Learn to swim.` Another says, `Call my family every week.` A third says, `Make a new friend.`

Nobody knows who wrote each card. The cards become a new tradition.

Nora looks around the hall. Last year, she did not know these people. Now they share food, music, and a new calendar.

Her own card says, `Feel at home here.` She thinks that hope is already coming true.

## Book 4: The Gift without a Name

Target: present, wrap, label, belong, guess, surprise, open.
Recycle: colour, size, family, party.

At a family holiday dinner, there are seven wrapped gifts under a small tree. Each gift has a name label except one.

The nameless present is long, light, and covered in green paper. It came from a clothing shop. The paper style looks familiar, but nobody knows who it belongs to.

"Who brought it?" Dad asks. Everyone tries to guess.

They make one more guess about the special present.

Nobody remembers. A tall grandfather with a grey beard thinks it is for his wife. Grandma thinks it is for her, and Ben thinks it may be a toy.

They open the gifts with labels first. At the end, the green package is still there.

Mila notices a tiny letter `M` under the paper.

"Whose present is it?" Ben asks.

"Maybe it is mine," Mila says.

Inside is a blue umbrella and a note about a coat. The coat was too tight, so Mila can exchange it for a loose one. Her old umbrella broke last week.

Her brother suddenly remembers. "I wrapped it, but the label fell off."

The family laughs at the surprise. The gift was not a mystery because it was expensive or strange. It was a mystery because one small piece of paper was missing.

Mila writes her full name on the umbrella before using it.

## Book 5: A Party for the Whole Street

Target: celebrate, prepare, neighbour, table, share, enough, together.
Recycle: invitation, food, weather, music.

The people on Maple Street plan a summer party. Each building prepares something, and every neighbour can invite a guest. Nobody will refuse.

Children decorate the tables for this special holiday.

Building ten brings tables and chairs. Building twelve brings music. Building fourteen brings drinks. Nora's building prepares food.

Thirty people accept the invitation, but forty-two arrive.

"Do we have enough food?" Nora asks.

The neighbours cut large cakes into smaller pieces. They add bread, fruit, and salad from their homes. A cafe gives them a pot of soup.

Then rain begins. Everyone carries the tables into a community hall. The room is small, so people sit close together.

An older neighbour plays guitar. Children dance near the tables, while other guests play chess or kick a ball in the hall.

The street party does not happen on the street, and the guest number is wrong. Still, every person has food and a seat.

They watch small fireworks before midnight.

The neighbours celebrate together and share the work of cleaning the hall. They are tired but proud. Somebody asks, "Same place next summer?"

"Maybe," Nora says. "But we will check the weather first."
